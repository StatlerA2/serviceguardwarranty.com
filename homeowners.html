If Something Goes Wrong, You're Covered

Roof leaks from installation errors can appear months or years later. With a standard warranty, you'd have to track down the roofer and hope they'll fix it.

With a Service Guard warranty, the carrier is legally obligated to pay valid claims — even if the original roofing company is no longer in business.

Planning to sell your home? If your Service Guard warranty is still active at the time of sale, it transfers to the new owner automatically — no extra paperwork, no additional cost. That means your roof investment adds real, documented value that buyers can see.

Built Around Workmanship Risk

Service Guard is designed to address workmanship-related roof issues after installation. It is not a substitute for homeowners insurance and is not intended to cover storm, hail, wind, or other weather-related damage. This distinction helps homeowners understand what to expect and helps contractors offer a clear, responsible warranty product.

Homeowner Questions

A manufacturer warranty covers defects in the shingles or roofing materials themselves. A workmanship warranty covers errors in how the roof was installed — things like improper flashing, bad sealing, or incorrect ventilation.
